# Cosine Similarity vs Dot Product vs Euclidean Distance

<!-- claim: [STANDARD KNOWLEDGE] Cosine similarity, dot product, and Euclidean distance are mathematically linked when embedding vectors are unit-normalized. -->
:::callout[note]{title="TL;DR"}
Vector databases evaluate similarity using three primary distance metrics: Cosine Similarity, Dot Product, and Euclidean (L2) Distance. While conceptually distinct, modern embedding models (like OpenAI `text-embedding-3`) produce unit-normalized vectors ($\|v\| = 1$). Under unit normalization, Cosine Similarity and Dot Product become mathematically identical, allowing vector indexes to run fast hardware-accelerated matrix multiplications without square-root division overhead.
:::

## The Three Distance Metrics Defined

Given two vectors $\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v} \in \mathbb{R}^d$:

```
        ▲ Vector u
        │  \
        │   \  Angle θ (Cosine measures θ)
        │    \
        │     \
        │      ▼ Vector v
        └───────────────────────▶
```

### 1. Cosine Similarity (Angular Proximity)
Measures the cosine of the angle between two vectors, completely independent of their lengths:

$$\text{Cosine}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{\|\mathbf{u}\| \|\mathbf{v}\|} = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^d u_i v_i}{\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^d u_i^2} \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^d v_i^2}}$$

- **Value Range**: $[-1.0, 1.0]$ (where $1.0$ is identical orientation, $0.0$ is orthogonal, $-1.0$ is diametrically opposite).

### 2. Dot Product / Inner Product (MIPS)
Multiplies corresponding dimensions and sums the results, incorporating both angle and magnitude:

$$\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v} = \sum_{i=1}^d u_i v_i = \|\mathbf{u}\| \|\mathbf{v}\| \cos(\theta)$$

### 3. Euclidean Distance / L2 Distance (Geometric Separation)
Measures the straight-line distance between the endpoints of two vectors:

$$L_2(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^d (u_i - v_i)^2}$$

- **Value Range**: $[0, \infty)$ (where $0.0$ indicates identical coordinates).

---

## The Normalization Equivalence Theorem

Most modern embedding models (including OpenAI, Cohere, and Hugging Face sentence-transformers) automatically **L2-normalize** output vectors to unit length:

$$\|\mathbf{u}\| = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad \|\mathbf{v}\| = 1$$

When vectors have unit length ($\|\mathbf{u}\| = 1$ and $\|\mathbf{v}\| = 1$), the denominator in Cosine Similarity disappears:

$$\text{Cosine}(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \frac{\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}}{(1)(1)} = \mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}$$

Furthermore, Euclidean distance simplifies directly into a function of the dot product:

$$L_2^2(\mathbf{u}, \mathbf{v}) = \|\mathbf{u}\|^2 + \|\mathbf{v}\|^2 - 2(\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}) = 1 + 1 - 2(\mathbf{u} \cdot \mathbf{v}) = 2 - 2 \cos(\theta)$$

<!-- claim: [STANDARD KNOWLEDGE] For unit-normalized vectors, rankings generated by Cosine Similarity, Dot Product, and Euclidean Distance are strictly identical. -->
**The Takeaway**: For unit-normalized embeddings, Cosine Similarity, Dot Product, and Euclidean Distance produce **strictly identical nearest-neighbor rankings**.

## Performance Implications in Vector Databases

If all three metrics produce identical rankings for normalized vectors, why does vector index configuration matter?

:::compare{left="Dot Product (Inner Product)" right="Cosine Similarity (Unnormalized)"}
**Computational Cost: Low**
- Requires only multiply-accumulate operations: $\sum u_i v_i$.
- Highly optimized on modern CPUs (AVX-512) and GPUs (tensor cores).
- **Recommended for production indexes** when embeddings are pre-normalized.
===
**Computational Cost: Higher**
- Requires calculating square roots and dividing by vector magnitudes for every candidate vector during search.
- Necessary only when vectors have variable lengths that carry semantic weight.
:::

## Key Takeaways

- **Cosine measures angle**: It ignores vector length and evaluates directional alignment.
- **Unit normalization creates equivalence**: When vectors have length 1, Cosine, Dot Product, and L2 produce identical rankings.
- **Prefer Dot Product for ANN indexing**: For pre-normalized vectors, Dot Product runs faster by eliminating magnitude divisions.
- **Ensure vectors are normalized**: Verify that your embedding pipeline normalizes vectors before writing to vector indexes.
